- **My week: a goal in days.** (2026-11-18: the Sun–Sat week strip is built on the home page; what is left of this idea is the goal the student chooses, "2 of 3 days this week".) The student picks how many days a week to practise (2, 3 or 5); the home page shows Sun–Sat as dots, filled for the days practised, and "2 of 3 days this week". Seen at: Khan Academy's advice to choose fixed study days and a goal (https://blog.khanacademy.org/keeping-your-streak-alive-insights-tips-from-the-last-6-months/); Languagenut suggests weekly streak challenges (https://help.languagenut.com/article/343-student-streaks). Why: a school student with exams and homework cannot always manage every day; a weekly goal he chose himself is reachable, and does not punish one missed day the way a pure streak does. Size **S**. Nothing paid, no accounts, no decision needed (use a Sunday-first week for Saudi schools).
- **Practice days on the teacher's Students page.** Add "streak" and "days practised this week" to the progress summary each student already sends, and show them as a column next to "last practised". Seen at: Languagenut presents streaks as a sign of effort for teachers (https://help.languagenut.com/article/343-student-streaks); Khan Academy encourages teachers to run streak challenges in class (https://blog.khanacademy.org/keeping-your-streak-alive-insights-tips-from-the-last-6-months/). Why: the teacher can praise steady effort, not only high scores — a weak student who practises four days a week deserves to be noticed. Size **S–M** (two more numbers inside the saved summary; no database change expected). Nothing paid; no accounts. Note: like the rest of the summary, the numbers come from the student's device and are not verified.
- **Class goal: everyone's practice adds up.** The teacher sets one target for the class (for example 200 finished activities this month) and optionally writes the reward; each student sees a bar "Our class: 124 / 200" that every student's practice fills, and a celebration when it is reached. Seen at: Khan Academy's class challenges where each student's points go to a shared class goal and the teacher chooses the reward (https://blog.khanacademy.org/five-ways-khan-academy-is-making-student-practice-more-motivating/); Duolingo's shared quests between friends (https://blog.duolingo.com/friends-quests/); Pearson recommends team goals over individual ranking (https://www.pearson.com/languages/en-ia/community/blogs/gamification-techniques-teaching-3-26.html). Why: it is motivation without a ranking table — nobody is shown at the bottom, and weaker students help the class as much as strong ones. Size **M** (the server must store the goal and return a class total to students; probably one small new table). Nothing paid, no student accounts. **Needs the owner's decision**: is a class total visible to students acceptable, and who chooses the reward.
<!-- 2026-11-18 researcher — angle: vocabulary and spaced repetition (bringing words back before they are forgotten). Longer notes in docs/research.md. -->
- **Practise my mistakes.** The site remembers, on the device, the items a student got wrong (a wrong pair in a lesson's Match game, a phrase not said clearly in Speak, a wrong answer in a listening exercise) and offers a short round of up to ten of them from the home page and My progress: "Practise my mistakes (7)". An item leaves the list after it is answered correctly twice. Seen at: Duolingo's Practice tab, whose "Mistakes" session replays a learner's earlier errors (https://blog.duolingo.com/guide-to-duolingo-practice-hub/); Busuu repeats the words a learner keeps forgetting until they are recalled (https://www.busuu.com/en/languages/spaced-repetition). Why: today a wrong answer is only counted and then forgotten by the site; the words a student gets wrong are exactly the ones worth ten more minutes, and a list that gets shorter is a clear, reachable goal. Size **M** (S if the first version covers only the Match game and lesson Speak). Nothing paid, no accounts, no decision needed. The list lives on the device like the rest of a student's progress.
- **Words to review today.** Every lesson phrase a student has practised gets a "next review" date on the device, with growing gaps: the next day, then 3, 7, 14 and 30 days; a correct answer moves it one step on, a wrong one sends it back to the first step. The home "today" card shows "8 words to review" and opens a five-minute round built from what the site already has (hear the phrase and choose its picture, see the picture and choose the words). Seen at: Memrise describes fixed steps from hours to months, with a wrong answer returning the item to the first step (https://www.memrise.com/blog/february-product-roundup); Duolingo explains why spaced reviews beat repeating a word many times in one evening, and chooses review items by time and accuracy (https://blog.duolingo.com/spaced-repetition-for-learning); Lingvist times each repeat for just before a word would be forgotten (https://lingvist.com/blog/spaced-repetition-in-learning/). Why: a finished lesson is never seen again here, so its words fade within a week or two; a small daily review is also a natural "one thing to do today" for the streak. Size **M**. Nothing paid, no accounts, no decision needed. Keep the round short and capped (for example 10 items) so a student back after a holiday is not met by a pile of 80.
- **Mixed match: one minute, all my lessons.** A timed matching round that mixes pictures and words from every lesson the student has finished, not only the open one; it shows the number matched in 60 seconds and the student's own best, never other students' scores. Seen at: Duolingo's timed word-matching challenge and its "Words" practice built on matching (https://blog.duolingo.com/ways-to-practice-in-duolingo, https://blog.duolingo.com/guide-to-duolingo-practice-hub/). Why: the Match game exists already but only inside one lesson, where the four pictures are quickly known by position; mixing lessons makes old words come back, and beating your own record is a reason to play again. Size **S** (reuses the Match game's drawing and the lessons' pairs). Nothing paid, no accounts, no decision needed. Needs at least two finished lessons to be worth showing.
- **My words.** A page that lists every word and phrase from the lessons a student has finished, grouped by level, each with its picture and a 🔊 button, and a small mark the site sets by itself: new, learning, known (from how the student did in Match, Speak and the review rounds). A counter at the top: "You know 23 words". Seen at: Busuu's vocabulary trainer, which saves the words met in lessons automatically and shows which are weak (https://www.busuu.com/en/languages/spaced-repetition); Cambridge's free A1 activities include a picture dictionary with pronunciation practice (https://assets.cambridgeenglish.org/portal/learner/a1/vocabulary.html); the British Council's vocabulary pages teach meaning, pronunciation and spelling together (https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/vocabulary). Why: percentages on My progress say little to a 13-year-old; "23 words I know" is something he can see grow and show a parent, and the page is a quick place to revise before a school test. Size **S** for the list with pictures and sound, **M** with the marks. Nothing paid, no accounts, no decision needed. Works best after "Practise my mistakes" or "Words to review today" exist, since they supply the marks.
- **Words of the week from the teacher.** In teacher mode, a teacher ticks up to ten phrases from the lessons as this week's words; they travel in the course file students already load, and each student's home page offers "This week's words: practise 3 today" until all ten have been answered correctly. Seen at: Cambridge English's advice to families to keep a box of the week's new words and take a few out each day to go over again (https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/learning-english/parents-and-children/your-childs-interests/practising-vocabulary-outside-the-classroom/); the British Council's children's site organises word practice as small sets on one subject (https://learnenglishkids.britishcouncil.org/grammar-vocabulary/word-games). Why: it ties the site to what the class is doing in school this week, which is the strongest reason a student has to open it, and it is a few words a day rather than a whole lesson. Size **M**. Nothing paid, no accounts. **Needs the owner's decision**: it only works if teachers really publish a course file (`course.json`) each week — otherwise the weekly words would need to be stored on the class server, which is a bigger change.
